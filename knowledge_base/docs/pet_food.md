# Pet Food Guidelines

## Puppy nutrition
Puppies need food formulated for growth, not adult maintenance food. Look for
labels stating "complete and balanced for growth" or "all life stages" (AAFCO
or FEDIAF certified). Large-breed puppies (expected adult weight over 50 lb,
e.g. Labrador, Golden Retriever, German Shepherd) need a large-breed puppy
formula with controlled calcium/phosphorus to prevent skeletal problems.
Feed 3-4 small meals a day until 6 months old, then 2 meals a day.

## Adult dog food
Adult dogs do well on maintenance formulas matched to their activity level.
High-energy or working dogs benefit from higher-protein, higher-fat formulas
(around 25-30% protein). Sedentary or overweight dogs do better on
weight-management formulas with more fiber and fewer calories per cup.

## Senior dog food
Dogs over 7 years (large breeds) or 10 years (small breeds) often benefit
from senior formulas with joint-support additives (glucosamine, chondroitin),
lower calorie density, and easily digestible protein.

## Cat nutrition
Cats are obligate carnivores and need higher protein than dogs, plus taurine
(an amino acid cats cannot synthesize -- deficiency causes heart and eye
problems). Wet food helps with hydration since cats have a low thirst drive.
Kittens need kitten-formula food until about 12 months old.

## Feeding by weight/activity
A rough starting point for adult dogs is 25-30 calories per pound of body
weight per day, adjusted up for high activity and down for low activity or
weight-loss goals. Always check the specific product's feeding guide, since
calorie density varies a lot between brands.

## Foods to avoid
Chocolate, grapes/raisins, onions, garlic, xylitol (common in sugar-free
gum/peanut butter), and cooked bones are toxic or dangerous for dogs and
cats. Always confirm any new food or treat with a veterinarian, especially
for pets with existing medical conditions.
